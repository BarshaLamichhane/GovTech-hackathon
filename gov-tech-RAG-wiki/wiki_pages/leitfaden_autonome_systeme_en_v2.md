# Report Summary
This document provides guidelines for regulatory questions concerning autonomous systems, developed within the scope of the Innovation Sandbox for Artificial Intelligence (AI). The sandbox is a test environment for AI projects involving public administration, industry, and research, designed to promote responsible innovation by addressing regulatory questions and enabling the use of novel data sources.

The guidelines aim to clarify legal questions for manufacturers in Switzerland, particularly those with limited regulatory know-how and resources. They focus on autonomous ground vehicles and systems for commercial purposes, potentially operating in public spaces. The core challenge addressed is that current regulation and standardization are not keeping pace with technological developments, leading to an unclear legal framework for manufacturers.

# AI Use Case
The guidelines focus on autonomous ground vehicles and systems for commercial purposes, potentially in operation in public spaces.

**Examples of included systems:**
*   Autonomous tractors and agricultural systems
*   Autonomous lawn mowers for professional greenkeeping
*   Autonomous cleaning robots for real estate management
*   Autonomous security robots for patrolling

**Excluded systems:**
*   Autonomous systems operating in the air, on rails, or in water.
*   Autonomous systems used exclusively for private or purely research purposes.
*   Autonomous systems in operation exclusively on private property.
*   Autonomous systems that primarily transport goods or persons on public roads.
*   Medical applications and systems.

# Legal or Regulatory Issues
*   **Pace of Regulation:** Regulation and standardization are not keeping pace with technological developments, resulting in an insufficiently clear legal framework.
*   **Safety:** Ensuring the safety of the system is paramount; autonomous systems must not pose danger to humans (e.g., collisions).
*   **Product Safety:** Autonomous systems are generally considered "products" under the Federal Law on Product Safety. The Ordinance for Machine Safety (MaschV) often applies, along with other product-specific sectorial decrees and energy efficiency regulations.
*   **Market Entry & Certification:**
    *   Conformity assessment procedure is necessary (can be done by manufacturer or accredited testing institute).
    *   Issuance of a signed certificate of conformity after assessment.
    *   Risk assessment must be performed and inform machine construction.
    *   External certification (e.g., by SUVA, TÜV) is not mandatory but offers high certainty of legal adherence.
    *   CE conformity marking is not generally required for Switzerland but is useful if planning market entry in the EU.
*   **Public Road Operation:** Additional requirements exist for autonomous systems traveling on public roads, including authorization pursuant to the Road Traffic Act [Strassenverkehrsgesetz (SVG)].
*   **Data Protection:** Correct handling of personal data is crucial, particularly when systems are equipped with cameras.
*   **Liability:** Awareness of impending liability and protection from liability risks (especially for personal injury and property damage) is important, often requiring insurance.
*   **Standards:** Compliance with standards from international organizations (e.g., CEN/CENELEC, ISO/IEC) is relevant. Some CEN/CENELEC standards are mandatory specifications of EU legal texts.
*   **Market Control:** Responsibility for ensuring compliance with market entry rules rests with various bodies, such as Suva for machines used in companies.
*   **Documentation:** Documentation of all regulatory-relevant activities and adherence to safety-relevant regulations, norms, directives, ordinances, and standards is required.
*   **Agreements:** Definition of rights and duties of all parties involved in contracts and/or operating manuals.

# Technical Challenges
*   **Rapid Technological Progress:** The development of autonomous systems is advancing at an extraordinary pace.
*   **Standardization Lag:** The pace of technological development runs ahead of the publication of standards, meaning suitable standards are often not yet available for innovative products.
*   **Complexity of AI:** Autonomous systems based on advanced machine learning methods (e.g., deep neural networks) make decisions independently in complex situations, posing unique regulatory and safety challenges.

# Organizational Challenges
*   **Limited Regulatory Know-how:** Manufacturers often have limited know-how and resources to dedicate to clarifying regulatory aspects.
*   **Keeping Up with Standards:** It is challenging for manufacturers to keep up to date with the latest standards and to assess which standards are relevant and applicable.
*   **Documentation Burden:** Full documentation, quality assurance, and certification can slow the pace of product development.
*   **Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** Ensuring and improving a regulatory environment for responsible innovation requires interdisciplinary collaboration across different topics and institutions.

# Lessons Learned
*   **Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** Close collaboration between public administration, industry, and research is essential for clarifying regulatory questions and promoting responsible innovation.
*   **Peer Interaction:** Regular interaction with other manufacturers in the same sector facing similar regulatory questions can be highly valuable.
*   **Value of External Certification:** While costly, external certification offers a relatively high level of certainty that an autonomous system adheres to legal requirements.
*   **Continuous Monitoring:** Regulatory frameworks and standards are dynamic and will continually change, requiring manufacturers to closely monitor these developments.

# Recommendations
*   **Prioritize Action Points:** Manufacturers should prioritize the most important regulatory action points based on their specific system requirements.
*   **Monitor Developments:** Closely monitor developments in regulatory frameworks and standards.
*   **Consider CE Certification:** If planning to market in the EU, consider striving for CE certification from the start, even if not strictly required for Switzerland.
*   **Active Participation in Standardization:** Become actively involved in devising new standards (e.g., in mirror committees or work groups).
*   **Implement Risk Management:** Perform a professional risk management process to ensure adherence to important requirements, including risk assessment during machine construction.
*   **Ensure Documentation and Quality Assurance:** Have the knowledge and resources for full documentation, quality assurance, and certification of products.

# Report Summary
This document outlines legal best practices for implementing AI in education, derived from an **Innovation Sandbox for Artificial Intelligence** project in the Canton of Zurich. It focuses on a use case where students used a smartphone app to automatically correct handwritten math and spelling exercises. The guidelines address regulatory challenges (e.g., data protection, copyright) and provide recommendations for AI solution providers and schools. While tailored to Zurich’s legal framework, the insights are broadly applicable to other Swiss cantons.

---

# AI Use Case
- **Automated correction of handwritten student work**: A smartphone app scans and evaluates handwritten math/spelling exercises, reducing teachers’ administrative burden.
- **Key features**:
  - Individualized feedback for students.
  - Support for repetitive tasks (e.g., grading).

---

# Sector / Domain
- **Education**: Primary/elementary schools (Volksschulen) in Switzerland.
- **Public administration**: Cantonal and municipal bodies (e.g., Department of Education, data protection authorities).

---

# Data Used
- **Handwritten student work**: Worksheets with potential personal data (e.g., names, handwriting patterns).
- **Metadata**: Timestamps, exercise results, or identifiers (e.g., QR codes for anonymized access).
- **Legal considerations**:
  - Handwriting may qualify as personal data if identifiable (e.g., by teachers or AI providers with additional context).
  - Data minimization is critical (e.g., avoid collecting birthdates unless necessary).

# Legal or Regulatory Issues
1. **Data Protection**:
   - **Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP)**: Applies to private providers and federal authorities.
   - **Cantonal laws**: Zurich’s *Information and Data Protection Act (IDG)* governs public schools.
   - **Key requirements**:
     - Lawful basis for processing (e.g., school laws or ordinances).
     - Contractual obligations for third-party providers (e.g., AI tools).
     - Compliance with principles like proportionality, purpose limitation, and transparency.

2. **Copyright**:
   - Potential issues if AI tools use copyrighted educational materials without permission.

3. **Administrative/School Law**:
   - Cantonal *Volksschulgesetze* (elementary school laws) may include data processing provisions.
   - Municipal decrees or internal school directives may apply.

4. **Contract Law**:
   - Clarifying roles/responsibilities between AI providers and schools (e.g., liability for data breaches).

---

# Technical Challenges
- **Data anonymization**: Ensuring handwritten work cannot be linked to individual students (e.g., avoiding names on worksheets).
- **Privacy by Design/Default**:
  - Default settings should minimize data collection (e.g., guest access via QR codes).
  - Data storage limited to necessary duration.
- **Security**: Protecting against unauthorized access to student data (e.g., encryption, access controls).

---

# Organizational Challenges
- **Stakeholder coordination**:
  - Involving teachers, school management, ICT officers, and cantonal authorities early.
  - Aligning AI tools with existing school policies and cantonal regulations.
- **Legal complexity**:
  - Navigating overlapping federal/cantonal laws and municipal directives.
  - Clarifying liability (e.g., schools remain responsible even when outsourcing data processing).
- **Trust and transparency**:
  - Ensuring students/parents understand how data is used (e.g., fulfilling duty to provide information).

---

# Lessons Learned
1. **Holistic approach**: Legal, technical, and organizational aspects must be addressed collaboratively.
2. **Early involvement**: Engaging data protection authorities and education departments reduces risks.
3. **Data minimization**: Avoid collecting unnecessary personal data to simplify compliance.
4. **Privacy by Design**: Integrate data protection principles from the outset (e.g., anonymization, limited storage).
5. **Clear contracts**: Define roles, responsibilities, and liability between schools and AI providers.

---

# Recommendations
1. **For AI Providers**:
   - Conduct joint legal assessments with schools to identify applicable laws.
   - Design tools to minimize personal data (e.g., anonymized access, short retention periods).
   - Assist schools in fulfilling transparency obligations (e.g., providing information for data subjects).

2. **For Schools**:
   - Consult cantonal education departments and data protection authorities before implementation.
   - Ensure AI tools comply with cantonal data protection laws (e.g., IDG in Zurich).
   - Establish clear contracts with providers, including data processing agreements.

3. **For Policymakers**:
   - Clarify legal frameworks for AI in education (e.g., harmonize cantonal regulations).
   - Promote sandboxes to test AI solutions in controlled environments.
